# The Space Janitor

Earth's blue curve drifts silently beyond the glass as stars wheel past.
A faint coolant hiss echoes as the janitor's cart squeaks past.
The robot mop — Dusty — smells of ozone-laced antiseptic with a faint, metallic tang of starlight.
The station's emergency alarm sounds like a sharp, pulsating two-tone klaxon; Friday is the janitor's favorite day, when zero-gravity mop-up finally gets a quiet week's end.
Lunch is floating peanut butter sandwiches, and the radio plays lo-fi cosmic funk all shift.
Earth glows below, a tapestry of golden city lights and swirling clouds.
A message in the bathroom stall reads: "For a good time, call the duty officer, extension 4471."
A sneeze propels him backward while mucus droplets drift everywhere.
In the airlock, a single red rubber duck floats — the stern retired admiral who commands the station has no comment.
The recycling unit composts old coffee grounds into nutrient-rich soil.
A small crescent moon inked on his wrist glows faintly.
Come New Year, crewmates share a special meal and call family.
He fears vacuum leaks that steal his broom, but plans to retire on a quiet asteroid farm.
And as the lights go out: at least the floors are finally clean.
